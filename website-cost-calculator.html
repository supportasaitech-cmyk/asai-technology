What actually drives the price

Page count is the obvious one, but it's rarely the biggest factor. Functionality moves the number far more: a store, a booking flow or a payment gateway each add real engineering. Design depth matters too — a clean standard build versus a fully custom interface is a genuine difference in hours, not just polish.

What should not inflate a quote is agency overhead. You're paying for the site, not someone's office.

Two questions worth asking any developer

"Do I own the code and accounts?" — if the answer is anything other than a clear yes, you're renting your own website. "Is it hand-coded or a page builder?" — builders are quicker to produce and permanently slower to load, which quietly costs you search visibility and conversions.
