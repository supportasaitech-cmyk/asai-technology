How much time can automation really save?

Most small businesses recover between 5 and 20 hours a month from their first few automations. The saving rarely comes from one dramatic change — it comes from removing four or five small jobs that quietly repeat every week.

What should a small business automate first?

Start with the task that is repetitive, rule-based, done often, and easy to get wrong. If it follows the same steps every time and needs no judgement, it can almost certainly be automated.

  • Lead follow-up — the highest-value one, because slow replies lose sales outright
  • Invoicing and payment reminders — frequent, predictable, and tied directly to cash
  • Moving data between apps — any copy-paste between a form, a sheet and a tool
  • Answering the same questions — the ones you type out several times a week

Is automation expensive to set up?

It does not need to be. A single automation that removes three hours of manual work a week returns roughly 150 hours a year — which usually pays for itself within weeks. You do not need enterprise software to begin.

Why the number above is usually an underestimate

This calculator only counts the minutes spent doing the task. It does not count the cost of context switching — stopping other work to handle something manual — or the sales lost when a follow-up goes out two days late. Those are real, and they're larger than the raw hours.
